Self-contained skill. Everything needed to run Intempt's affiliate and agency outreach
program end to end. Combines email marketing, outbound pipeline, and Sid's voice rules.

## 1. THE CORE STRATEGY

**Give product first. Never pitch the affiliate program.**

Sid's exact framing:
- "Make friends and then influence people. Never in the other order."
- "The party is in our house. The food is free and the drinks are free."
- "Your goal is word of mouth. Their goal is flat fee. So don't lead with that."

The email offers free Intempt Pro access to a selected group.
The email does NOT mention: affiliate program, 30% recurring, commissions, Stripe,
referral discounts, or the word "partner" as a transactional offer.

**Personalize — Opener**
```
Based on {{LinkedIn Profile URL}}, {{Company Website}}, and the research fields above,
write TWO sentences (combined max 27 words) for the opening of a cold email.

Sentence 1: A hyper-specific observation from their LinkedIn posts, portfolio, or a
trigger event — something only someone who actually looked would know. Max 15 words.
Must start with "I" (saw/noticed/found) or be a direct question ("Quick one:").

Sentence 2: The pain implication — the exact problem that comes from what you observed
in sentence 1. Max 12 words. Do not explain or qualify — name the problem directly.

THE OPENER MUST DO TWO THINGS:
1. Reference something SPECIFIC about this person (signal you actually looked)
2. Bridge to a PAIN — name or imply a problem they almost certainly have

DO NOT write a pure compliment or observation with no pain bridge. Telling someone
"your CRO teardowns are great" gives them nothing new. They already know their work
is good. The opener earns a reply when they think: "how did they know that's exactly
my problem?"

Use ONE of these three types, in priority order:

TYPE 1 — PAIN BRIDGE OBSERVATION
You noticed something specific about their work, setup, or clients that implies a
pain Intempt solves. The observation is specific; the implication names the pain.
Format: "I [saw/noticed/came across] [specific thing] — [the pain that comes with it]."
Examples:
- "Noticed you're running lifecycle for DTC clients across three separate Klaviyo accounts — that segmentation layer gets messy fast."
- "Saw you manage CRM migrations for B2B SaaS clients — the data unification piece is usually where those projects stall."
- "I keep seeing CRO agencies running A/B tests without a proper CDP underneath — curious if attribution is hitting you the same way."

TYPE 2 — TRIGGER + PAIN
Something that just happened signals a specific pain they're about to feel or are
already feeling.
Format: "Saw/Noticed you [specific trigger] — [the pain that typically follows]."
Examples:
- "Noticed you're hiring a lifecycle specialist — usually means the manual segmentation work is outpacing the current stack."
- "Saw you just landed a new ecommerce brand — running their lifecycle without centralized customer data is the hard part."
- "Noticed you posted about Klaviyo's segmentation limits — that ceiling hits faster when you're managing multiple brand accounts."

TYPE 3 — PAIN QUESTION
A direct question about a workflow pain they almost certainly have. Questions get a
mental "yes" before they read your pitch — that yes makes everything after land harder.
Format: "Quick one — [specific question about a pain Intempt solves for their work]?"
Examples:
- "Quick one — when you're building segments for multiple clients, are you doing that separately in each ESP or did you find a way to centralize it?"
- "Quick one — are you still manually rebuilding journey logic per client, or did you find a way to template that across accounts?"
- "Quick one — when a client asks for a cross-channel view of their customer data, where are you pulling that from right now?"

HARD RULES:
- Sentence 1 must contain "I" OR be a direct question ("Quick one:")
- Sentence 1: max 15 words. Sentence 2: max 12 words. Combined: max 27 words.
- Must come from real research (posts, portfolio, trigger, tool) — not from their bio
- No bio recaps ("You founded X to do Y" / "You've been doing CRO since...")
- No pure compliments with no bridge ("your teardown was great" / "loved your post")
- No city or location references
- No company name wordplay
- No generic phrases ("I came across your profile", "I love what you're building")
- Must sound like a DM you'd send a colleague who has this exact problem

Output both sentences only. No labels, no explanations.
```

## 6. EMAIL FORMAT (v7, current)

**Why v6 stopped working:**
Open rates are low because subject lines aren't specific enough. Reply rates are low
because the email body still feels like a campaign — a generic pain + 3 generic
capabilities. The prospect reads it and feels like one of hundreds. They're right.

The fix: deep research before writing, one hyper-specific capability instead of three
generic ones, and exclusivity framing that makes them feel genuinely hand-picked.

---

### PRE-WRITE RESEARCH CHECKLIST (mandatory — do this before every email)

Before writing a single word, look up and note:

1. **LinkedIn posts (last 3-5)**: What are they actively talking about? A specific tool
   frustration, a client type they mention, a workflow they describe, a pain they post about?
   This is the highest-value research. If they posted about a specific problem, that's your opener.

2. **Website portfolio or case studies**: Which specific clients or industries do they feature?
   What service is front and center? Are there specific results or projects they're proud of?

3. **Career trigger**: Did they go from in-house to fractional recently? Launch a new service?
   Hire someone? Win an award? These are your trigger event subject lines and openers.

4. **Tool signals**: What tools do they mention or certify in? Klaviyo Partner, HubSpot Partner,
   Shopify Partner? This tells you exactly which Intempt module to pitch.

5. **Client type**: Who do they actually work with? Not their job title — their clients.
   A "creative director" working with DTC brands is a different email than one working with
   enterprise B2B. The client type defines the specific capability to pitch.

**Output of research:** One specific thing (post, case, trigger, tool) + one pain + one
Intempt feature that directly solves it. Write from these three inputs only.

---

### CRITICAL RULE — PITCH ONE FEATURE ONLY

After researching, identify the ONE Intempt feature that maps to what they actually
deliver to clients every day. Never list multiple features. Never say "lifecycle
automation, experiments, analytics, and an AI creative studio." That is pitching
everything and connecting to nothing.

**The test:** What does this person physically deliver to a client? A landing page?
An email flow? A data migration? An A/B test? An analytics dashboard? Match that
deliverable to one Intempt feature and pitch only that.

**Feature mapping by segment:**

| Segment | What they actually deliver to clients | The ONE feature to pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Creative agencies / web designers | Landing pages, websites, brand creative | Intempt Studio — build landing pages in minutes, all client brands in one place |
| Email marketing agencies | Email flows, campaigns, automation | Intempt Journeys + AI email creative |
| CRO agencies | A/B tests, landing page experiments | Intempt Experiments |
| Lifecycle marketing consultants | Customer journeys, retention flows | Intempt Journeys |
| CRM/CDP consultants | Data unification, CRM setup, segmentation | Intempt Data Hub + AI Segmentation |
| Marketing automation agencies | HubSpot/Klaviyo builds, automation workflows | Intempt Journeys + Workflows |
| RevOps consultants | CRM, pipeline, revenue operations | Intempt Pipeline + Workflows |
| Sales enablement consultants | Sales processes, CRM, meeting intelligence | Intempt Meetings + Pipeline |
| DTC agency owners | Acquisition + retention + lifecycle for DTC brands | Intempt Journeys + AI creative + Analytics |
| Fractional CMOs | Full marketing stack decisions across clients | Full platform (they choose tools for all clients — show them the whole picture) |

**Bad (pitching everything):**
"lifecycle automation, experiments, analytics, and an AI creative studio in one place"

**Good (pitching one thing):**
"Intempt Studio, an AI creative tool that lets you build landing pages for clients in
minutes — all your client brands in one place"

**Another good example:**
"Intempt Journeys, a lifecycle automation builder — your clients' flows and campaigns
in one platform instead of one ESP per brand"

---

### CRITICAL RULE — NEVER RESTATE WHAT THEY DO

They know what they do. Opening a sentence with "You run lifecycle marketing for
DTC clients" or "As a fractional CMO" signals template immediately. It wastes their
attention on information they already have.

Instead: open on something they do NOT already know — a pain implication, a trigger
you noticed, a specific observation from their work. See the opener types in Section 5.

### THE TWO-TOUCH SEQUENCE (Ramp Method) — Use this for every batch

Inspired by how Ramp does outbound. Email 1 pitches. Email 2 comes the next day
with a "mistake" — they forgot to include something. The fake slip creates a human
moment, triggers the empathy reflex, and pulls replies from people who wouldn't
have responded to Email 1 alone.

Why it works:
- People are wired to reassure someone who made a small mistake
- It breaks the pattern of "obviously automated follow-up" sequences
- The drop in pressure in Email 2 makes it feel like a real person
- In a world full of AI emails, a slight disorganisation reads as refreshing

**EMAIL 1 — v9 format (see below). Must NOT include the link or setup details.**
End with "Want in?" — no link. The link is the "forgotten" thing in Email 2.

**EMAIL 2 — sent the next day**
Subject: "re: [exact same subject as Email 1]" — threads into the same conversation

Format:
```
[Name], shoot — forgot to include [the actual link / that there's no card or trial / one specific thing relevant to them].

[The thing they forgot: link OR "Worth saying: no card, no trial. Full Pro access from day one." OR one specific feature]

[One casual, research-specific line. Not a pitch. Just human curiosity about their situation.]

Somya / Sid
```

Rules for Email 2:
- Start with "Shoot" or "[Name], shoot" — the informal slip creates the human moment
- The "forgotten thing" must be REAL and useful (the link, the free detail, a feature)
- The final line is curious, not pitchy: "Genuinely curious how [specific thing from their research] is going."
- Total length: 2-3 sentences max. Shorter than Email 1.
- No em dashes
- No "just following up" or "circling back"
- No new product pitch — the pitch was Email 1

**What to "forget" — pick one:**
1. The actual link to set up Intempt Pro access (most natural — Email 1 says "Want in?" with no link)
2. That it is completely free with no trial expiry and no card needed
3. One specific feature that maps directly to something in their research

### THE CREATIVE IDEAS CAMPAIGN FORMAT (Primary — Email 1)

Source: Proven outbound strategy used by Clay, PR companies getting 80 leads/day,
and multiple YC-batch companies. Works because ideas are specific, tangible, and
show you understand the prospect's actual business — not AI slop compliments.

Flow: Hook → 2-3 specific ideas for how Intempt helps their exact situation → Offer

The ideas are the product. They are NOT generic capabilities ("Intempt can help with X").
They must reference their actual clients, actual tools, actual results from your research.

**Format:**
```
Hi [Name],

[One-line hook: what you noticed about their specific situation from research.]

Had some ideas on how Intempt could help with [their exact client type or situation]:

- [Idea 1: tangible, references their actual clients/tools/results — 15-20 words]
- [Idea 2: tangible, specific to their research — 15-20 words]
- [Idea 3: tangible — 15-20 words]

We're giving Pro free to a small group. [One Intempt context sentence specific to them.]

Want to see how it works?

Somya / Sid
```

**One-liner version (when you only have one strong idea):**
```
Hi [Name],

Had an idea for [their situation]: [one specific, tangible idea tied to their actual
research — what Intempt could do for their exact client or situation].

We're giving Pro free to a small group. [One Intempt context sentence]. Want to see it?

Somya / Sid
```

**What makes a good idea:**
- References their actual named clients or tools (not generic "your clients")
- Maps to a real Intempt capability: behavioral data, journeys, experiments, segmentation, creative studio
- Shows you thought about their specific situation, not just their job title
- Is immediately actionable — they can picture what this would look like for them

**Idea templates by segment:**

| Segment | Strong idea angles |
|---|---|
| DTC retention agencies | LTV dashboards per brand, post-purchase behavioral segments, subscription vs loyalty experiments |
| Creative agencies | Brand profile per client with post-launch behavioral data, narrative angle experiments, audience profile tracking |
| Full-service digital | Unified channel attribution view, behavioral tracking on client sites, automated follow-up journeys for non-converters |
| Fractional CMOs | Reusable journey templates across engagements, unified reporting across all clients, behavioral experiments per vertical |
| HubSpot/RevOps | Behavioral layer under CRM, revenue attribution dashboards, automated workflows triggered on real behavior |
| Email marketing agencies | Klaviyo + behavioral data connection, post-send behavioral segments, LTV visibility alongside campaign metrics |
| Shopify partners | Post-launch behavioral data feeding back into design, Shopify + email + paid in one view per client |

**Total length: 85-110 words** (slightly longer than v9 because the ideas carry the value)

**Subject lines:** Keep the research-specific format from v9. The "ideas" framing is in the body.

### THE V9 FORMAT (Backup — use when you only have 1 strong research signal)

Flow: Observation → Intempt solution → Offer → Outcome. No pivot language. No "here's
the thing though." Direct, short, moves fast. Reads like a note from someone who thought
of you when they saw a specific thing.

```
Hi [First Name],

[Opening: 1-2 short punchy sentences. State their specific situation directly from
research. Named clients, specific results, specific tool combination. No pivot phrase.
Just describe what you saw.]

Intempt [what it does, connected directly to their specific situation. One sentence.
Makes clear what the product is.]

We're giving Pro free to a small group. [Outcome: what they'd specifically get or
be able to do. One sentence. "You'd [verb] [specific thing]."]

Want in?

Somya / Sid
```

**Word count target: 55-75 words total.**

**What makes this different from v8:**
- Intempt context comes BEFORE the offer, not after it
- No pivot phrases ("here's the thing though", "here's the part that's usually missing")
- The observation and the Intempt solution sit next to each other, creating a direct problem-to-answer flow
- Shorter overall, reads faster

**Subject line format (v8):** `[2-5 word hook]` — NO first name in subject, NO em dashes
- All lowercase, no punctuation (comma between two brand names is fine)
- Must be exciting — implies a gap, a mystery, or a very specific result. Generic = ignored.
- Never use: first name, their company name, city, banned category words, em dashes

**4 subject line patterns that create excitement:**

1. **The gap pattern** — implies something is missing that they haven't noticed yet
   "what happens after the click" / "the part hubspot misses" / "after the brand launches" / "102m views, one gap"

2. **The specific result question** — references their actual result and implies you dug into it
   "what drove the 14x" / "what actually drove riverside" / "60% revenue, then what"

3. **The contrast** — two named things that create tension or curiosity
   "three tools, one client" / "rude health, shopify gap" / "nursery to law firm" / "nrg to nonprofit"

4. **The specific stat** — a number from their work used as an opener, creates "they looked at my stuff" feel
   "50m media, one blind spot" / "thirteen years, one gap" / "3 clients, 3 stacks"

NEVER write subjects that:
- Could apply to any agency ("marketing tips" / "a question" / "quick idea")
- Use banned words: campaigns, lifecycle, funnel, automation, growth, digital, email, strategy, marketing, scale, free, affiliate, partner, Intempt, demo, offer
- Use em dashes

**The pain pivot phrases (rotate these — don't repeat):**
- "Here's the thing though."
- "Here's the part that's usually missing."
- "Here's what I see consistently with that."
- "Here's where it gets tricky."
- "That's the tricky bit right there."

**Pain section rules:**
- Maximum 2 sentences. Not 3. Not 4.
- Must be specific to their research, not a generic pain
- Use their actual client names, case study stats, tool combination
- No bullets — flowing prose only

Bad: "Running multiple channels for clients means attribution is hard."
Good: "Three channels running at once for the same client, each in a different tool, no single view of what actually drove the florist's 14x ROAS."

**The Intempt context line (after the offer):**
After "We're giving Pro free to a small group right now." — add ONE sentence describing what Intempt does in plain language, specific to their situation. This is NOT a repeat of the benefit — it tells them WHAT Intempt is.

Templates to adapt:

- Full service / multi-channel: "Intempt connects all your client channels in one dashboard, runs automated journeys, and tracks what's actually driving results."

**The three numbers that matter:**
- Line 1 + 2: Maximum 27 words combined
- "One of about N": Use 15-20 for exclusivity. Not "a few", not "hundreds". A specific number.
- Email total: 70-90 words

### Subject Line Rules (v2 — research-updated)

**The core problem with the old approach:** describing what they do ("photography and paid",
"video to lead gen") tells them nothing they don't already know. They see it and move on.
What gets emails opened is CURIOSITY — either something that just happened (trigger), or
the sense that you specifically noticed something about them (asymmetry).

**4-tier priority framework (use the first tier that applies):**

**Tier 1 — Trigger event** (data: 54–68% open rate)
Reference something that just happened. The prospect opens because they want to know
what you saw, thought, or are connecting it to.
- Scan for: recent LinkedIn post, podcast appearance, new client announced, award,
  speaking slot, job posting for a specific role, press mention, rebranding, launch.
- Format: name the thing, 2-4 words, all lowercase.
- Examples: "the oatly win" / "saw your podcast" / "that attentive post" / "hiring a cro lead" / "the klaviyo award" / "your recent thread"

**Tier 2 — Information asymmetry** (data: 61–63% open rate)
Reference something specific you noticed about their work, stack, or model — without
explaining what you noticed. They open to find out what you saw.
- Scan for: a specific tool they're known for, a client result visible on their site,
  a workflow or model that's unusual, a retainer structure mentioned publicly.
- Format: "your [specific thing]" or "re: [their approach]" — 2-4 words, lowercase.
- Examples: "your klaviyo setup" / "re: the retention model" / "noticed your stack" / "your churn post" / "the vwo thing"

**Tier 3 — Named brand transition** (data: ~45–55% open rate, what we used before)
Two named companies or brands connected by context — their career move, a named client,
or a brand association. Named brands signal real research; descriptor words don't.
- Examples: "gymshark to adanola" / "capital one to fractional" / "logitech, four regions" / "durban to exeter"

**Tier 4 — Observable model** (data: ~35–45% open rate, fallback only)
Their specific, named service model when nothing better is available. Only use when
tiers 1-3 genuinely have no material to work from.
- Examples: "photography and paid" / "video to lead gen" / "search to lifecycle"

**Hard bans — any of these kills the open:**
- Their city or country alone ("manchester" / "toronto")
- First name alone (lazy default, not a signal)
- Their own company name (they know it)
- Category words: campaigns, lifecycle, funnel, automation, growth, digital, email, strategy, marketing, scale
- Selling words: free, opportunity, partner, affiliate, Intempt, demo, offer, check this out
- Anything that sounds like a subject line from a tool

**Format rules:** 2-4 words. All lowercase. No punctuation except a comma between two brand names. Never announce the pitch.

## 9. RESEARCH AND QUALIFICATION WORKFLOW

### Step 1: Qualify before researching

Disqualify immediately if any of these are true — do not spend research time on a bad lead:
- Full-time in-house employee at a single company (not a founder, owner, or fractional)
- Company headcount > 40 (for agencies) or > 15 (for solo consultants)
- Email domain is a personal ISP (gmail, hotmail, btinternet) — signals solopreneur or retired
- Influencer agency owner (flat fee model, not SaaS tools) — do not contact
- Life science, legal tech, GovCon, nonprofit, physical product compliance niches
- No overlap with any Intempt feature set
- Already in the contacted list — STOP, flag as SKIP

**DO NOT disqualify for:**
- Geography: Intempt is global. Netherlands, Germany, Australia, anywhere — do not disqualify on location.
- Being a solo operator or freelancer: solo designers, solo developers, solo consultants are valid leads.
  Pitch them differently — not about managing multiple clients, but about what Intempt does for THEIR
  work and their own client relationships. A solo web designer still builds brand profiles, tracks
  what drives referrals, and automates follow-up. The pitch just narrows to their personal workflow.

**Solo operator email format:**
Skip the multi-client framing. Focus on:
- What they can do for each project/client they work on
- Their own brand and portfolio visibility
- Automating the things they currently do manually

### Step 2: Deep research (mandatory before writing)

Do ALL of the following. The email quality depends on what you find here.

**LinkedIn profile:**
- Read their headline and current role — confirm they own or run the thing they're pitching
- Read their most recent 3-5 posts (not just what they posted, but what pain or situation they describe)
- Look at their featured section — often shows their best work or current focus
- Note any certifications: Klaviyo Partner, Shopify Partner, HubSpot Partner — these are tool signals

**Website:**
- Look at the portfolio or case studies page — which specific clients or brand names appear?
- Look at the services page — what's front and center vs buried?
- Look at the about page — what's their origin story? (former in-house → fractional is a signal)
- Look for any specific process or methodology they name (it tells you what they're proud of)

**Trigger moment (check all of these):**
- Did they post about a new client or project recently?
- Did they mention hiring or bringing on a collaborator?
- Did they win an award or get press coverage?
- Did they launch a new service or pricing model?
- Did they post a complaint or frustration about a tool?

### Step 3: Build the research note

Before writing, write down:
1. **The specific thing you found** (post, case, trigger, tool) — one sentence
2. **The pain it implies** — one sentence
3. **The ONE Intempt feature that solves it** — from the capability mapping table in Section 6
4. **Their exact descriptor** — the specific phrase you'll use in the exclusivity line

If you cannot answer all four, do more research. Do not write the email yet.

### Step 4: Write from the research note

- Line 1 of opener: from item 1 (the specific thing you found)
- Line 2 of opener: from item 2 (the pain)
- Capability line: from item 3 (one Intempt feature)
- Exclusivity descriptor: from item 4

The research note is the email. If the note is generic ("they run a creative agency for brands"),
the email will be generic. If the note is specific ("they posted last week about running three
separate Klaviyo accounts for their DTC clients and losing attribution"), the email will be specific.

## 15. ADVANCED OUTREACH TACTICS

### The Mirror Technique
People reply when they see themselves in your email. Not when they see your offer.

Instead of: "We help lifecycle marketers run better journeys."
Write: "Most lifecycle marketers I speak with are running flows in Klaviyo or Customer.io that stop the moment the automation ends — no signal of what happens after."

When someone reads a sentence that describes their situation better than they could describe it, they reply. That's the mirror. Build the opener and the outcome line around this.

### Micro-Yes Before Any Ask
Never ask for a meeting in a cold email. The brain treats "Got 15 minutes?" like a checkout page — same threat response.

"Want in?" works because it's frictionless. It's not a meeting. It's access. Get one small yes, then the next yes comes easier because people stay consistent with what they've already agreed to.

### One-Liner Follow-Up
If the first email gets no reply, follow up with a single question. No re-pitch, no value recap.

Format: "Hey [Name] — are you guys doing [specific thing]?"

Three reasons it works: doesn't look automated, specific enough to feel personal, low enough effort that replying takes three seconds. They mostly reply: "Yes / No / Not yet." — and that opens the conversation.

### Signal-Based Openers Only
Generic personalisation ("I came across your profile") has the same effect as no personalisation. Only reference things that show you actually looked: a company they came from, a named client, a certification, a transition, a post they wrote. The opener must make them think: "How did they know that?"

## 16. COLDIQ 5-STEP SYSTEM — APPLIED PRINCIPLES

Source: ColdIQ framework, validated across 300+ B2B outbound programs.

### ICP Precision Before Outreach
Do NOT start with 10,000-person lists. Build 200-500 precision accounts that match exactly on industry, company size, team structure, and ONE specific pain Intempt eliminates.

**ICP tiers by pain intensity, not firmographics.** A solo fractional CMO managing 5 clients on their own budget makes completely different buying decisions than the same title at a company where legal signs off. Same title, different urgency, different email.

**The 6-list model:** For each segment, break into 3 sub-profiles by buying behavior, map 2 personas per sub-profile (buyer + user). Run 3 copy variations per list. 18 tests total — this is how you find message-market fit in 1-3 campaigns instead of guessing for 6 months.

### Signal Stacking Before You Send
Outreach to accounts already researching your category converts at a completely different rate than cold outreach.

Stack at least 2 signals before any email goes out:
- **Repeat LinkedIn engagement** — liked 3+ posts in your category this month
- **G2/Capterra activity** — actively researching tools in your space
- **Job postings for roles you replace** — hiring signals tell you they're about to try what Intempt fixes
- **Recent funding** — changes budget authority, urgency, and team composition overnight

Timing is 90% of reply rate. Copy is 10%. Most teams flip this backwards and spend 90% of their time rewriting subject lines.

### Buying Committee: Parallel, Not Sequential
One person doesn't sign a B2B contract. An agency founder, their ops lead, and their finance contact all have a vote.

Old model: one person, five follow-ups, move on.
New model: identify 3-4 stakeholders per account, send each a DIFFERENT angle simultaneously.

When the founder doesn't reply but their head of client strategy forwards the email internally, the thread is alive. Single-threaded outreach never triggers those internal conversations.

For Intempt affiliate outreach: when targeting agencies with 10+ people, reach the founder AND their head of client work with different angle emails at the same time.

### Content-Outbound Calendar Sync
When a prospect receives an email, the first thing they do is look up the sender on LinkedIn.

Sync content calendar to outbound calendar:
- Running a campaign to lifecycle marketing consultants this month? Somya's and Sid's LinkedIn posts this month should be about lifecycle consultant problems.
- When a target prospect visits the profile after receiving the email, they see 3 posts written for their exact situation.

This turns a cold email into a warm one before they even decide to reply.

### Signal-Based Follow-Up (Not Schedule-Based)
Fixed-schedule sequences (day 3, day 7, day 12) ignore what the prospect is actually doing.

**Rule: if a prospect views your LinkedIn profile after email 1, follow up the SAME DAY. Not day 4.**

A profile view is a buying signal that is warm for 24 hours and cold for the rest of the week. Fixed-schedule follow-up consistently misses this window.

One-liner follow-up format (after a signal fires):
"Hey [Name] — are you guys doing [specific thing]?"

Three reasons it works: doesn't look automated, specific enough to feel personal, low effort to reply in 3 seconds. Replies come back: "Yes / No / Not yet" — and those open the conversation.

### Hybrid AI/Human Model
AI handles the top of funnel excellently: list building, enrichment, sequencing, autopilot follow-ups.
The moment a lead shows real intent (profile view, reply, content engagement), a human takes over.

Validated benchmark — 90-day test:
- Pure AI SDRs: 847 meetings booked, 11% converted to pipeline
- Hybrid (AI top-of-funnel, human on warm signals): 312 meetings, 38% conversion, 2x revenue with 63% fewer meetings

The gap is the case for human follow-up on warm signals. Volume is not the answer once signals fire.
